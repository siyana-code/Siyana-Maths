# UX Brief — MVP

**Status:** Draft for implementation review
**Audience:** Public students and the tutor/admin
**Primary device:** Mobile-first, with responsive desktop support

## 1. Design principles

- Make the answer useful even if a video cannot load.
- Keep the learning content in Sinhala; keep navigation and controls in English for the first release.
- Make the next action obvious: read the solution, study the marks, or watch the discussion.
- Use generous spacing and readable mathematical content rather than dense dashboard styling.
- Treat mobile performance and accessibility as first-class requirements.
- Never hide a missing video, failed embed, or unavailable provider without explaining it.

## 2. Information architecture

### Public navigation

- Home
- Past Papers
- Mathematics
- About
- Contact
- Admin sign-in (visually separated from student navigation)

### Public pages

#### Home

- concise tutor/platform introduction;
- primary CTA to browse past papers;
- latest or featured O/L Mathematics paper;
- explanation of answers, marking schemes, and external video discussions;
- contact CTA.

#### Past-paper catalog

- responsive list/grid of published papers;
- filters for year, paper number, medium, and subject;
- text search when enabled;
- empty/loading/error states;
- clear card labels and total marks.

#### Paper detail

Recommended order:

1. Paper title and metadata.
2. Total marks and publication information.
3. Paper-level video sources.
4. Ordered question navigation.
5. For each question:
   - question number and mark value;
   - question text/math;
   - Sinhala solution;
   - full marking-scheme steps;
   - question-specific video sources or a clear no-video message.
6. Contact CTA.

Do not hide answers behind a video or require an account.

#### About

- tutor identity and teaching focus;
- O/L Mathematics scope;
- how the platform is organized;
- links to social channels.

#### Contact

- WhatsApp button/deep link;
- phone;
- email;
- social links;
- accessible contact form;
- privacy/retention notice.

## 3. Admin experience

### Paper list

- title, year, paper number, medium, status, updated time;
- search and status filter;
- create-paper action;
- edit, preview, publish, archive, and delete actions.

### Paper editor

Use a sectioned editor rather than one giant form:

1. Paper details.
2. Questions and answers.
3. Marking scheme.
4. Video sources.
5. Preview and publishing.

For each question, keep the question, solution, marking-scheme items, and video sources visually grouped. Support reordering while keeping a stable database position.

### Marking-scheme editor

Each item should support:

- step number/order;
- method or step label;
- award/marking note;
- mark value;
- optional alternative-method label;
- Sinhala text/math content.

Show the question's calculated mark total beside the entered steps. Prevent publication when the total is invalid or unexplained.

## 4. Content rendering

- Use a controlled Markdown renderer.
- Render mathematics with a maintained KaTeX-compatible library.
- Sanitize all rendered HTML.
- Preserve line breaks and readable long-form Sinhala text.
- Provide a text fallback if an equation cannot render.
- Do not execute author-provided scripts, arbitrary HTML, or remote iframe URLs outside the provider allowlist.

## 5. Video presentation

For each source show:

- provider label;
- embed when available;
- direct “Watch on [provider]” link;
- a clear fallback message when embedding is unavailable.

Only allow known provider embed URLs generated by the backend. Never accept an arbitrary iframe URL from the admin form.

## 6. Responsive behavior

- Use a single-column reading flow on narrow screens.
- Keep question navigation and filters easy to reach without horizontal scrolling.
- Make video embeds responsive and avoid fixed-height overflow.
- Keep long marking-scheme steps expandable or clearly stacked on mobile.
- Ensure form controls have visible labels and useful error text.

## 7. Accessibility baseline

- Semantic headings and landmarks.
- Keyboard-operable filters, menus, dialogs, and form controls.
- Text alternatives for thumbnails and video actions.
- Visible focus states.
- Color contrast suitable for long reading and mathematical notation.
- Screen-reader-friendly question numbers, mark values, and status messages.

## 8. Visual direction

Start with a clean education-focused visual system:

- white/off-white reading surfaces;
- one trustworthy primary accent;
- restrained secondary colors for status and marks;
- clear typography with good Sinhala and Latin support;
- restrained use of cards and shadows;
- no heavy animation or distracting autoplay.

The final palette, logo, and brand name remain open.
